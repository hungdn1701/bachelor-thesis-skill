# From an idea to a proposal

A conversation, not a form. Use what the student already said, and do not ask it again.

## No idea yet

Ask one question at a time:

1. What have you built or studied that you would like to go deeper in?
2. What data, systems, users or hardware can you really get access to?
3. Has your advisor suggested a direction or a project?
4. How much time do you have, and what do you already know how to do?

When they have said enough to work from, continue below with their answer as the idea. If they still have nothing, send them to their advisor with these answers. Do not fill the gap with a topic of your own.

## With an idea

### 1. Understand

Restate the idea as one sentence: what problem, for whom. Then find out, asking at most five questions:

- Who has this problem, and how do we know it is real?
- What would count as success, and how would anyone tell?
- What can the student access: data, users, hardware, time, skills?
- What has been tried before?
- What did the advisor say?

### 2. Expand

Offer three to five variations of the student's idea, each with the reason it is worth considering.

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
| Narrow | What is the smallest version that still answers the question? |
| Question first | What would we need to measure to know this works? |
| Artifact | Would a system, a model, a dataset or a study show this best? |
| Type | Is the main result a working system (`build`) or an answer to a question (`research`)? |
| Audience | Is another group of users easier to reach, or more in need? |

### 3. Converge

Group what the student responds to into two or three directions, and compare them.

| Criterion | Ask |
|---|---|
| Value | Who benefits, and how much? |
| Feasibility | Is the hardest part possible with this time, data and skill? |
| Evaluable | Can it be measured before the defense? |
| Own work | What will be the student's own contribution? |

For each direction, name what it bets on, what could kill it, and what it ignores.

### 4. Write

Fill in `docs/proposal.md` once the student has chosen. If they asked for the file before choosing, write it with the options listed and the choice left open, and say so.

Then remind them to take it to their advisor and record the decision themselves.

## Signs an idea is weak

| Sign | Say |
|---|---|
| Only a list of features | What question does building this answer? |
| No way to evaluate it | How will you know it works, and compared with what? |
| Depends on data nobody has confirmed | Who will give you this data, and when? |
| Too broad for the time left | Which part would you keep if you had half the time? |
| Already solved, with nothing new | What will you add or show that was not there? |
| A target with no basis | Where does this number come from? |
