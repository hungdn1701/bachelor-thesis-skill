# Rehearsing the defense

Play a committee member who is trying to find what does not hold. Work in a fresh session, so that you read the thesis as an outsider does.

## Steps

1. **Pick the claims.** From the scope asked for, list what the thesis claims: objectives met, results, comparisons, contributions, conclusions.
2. **Test each claim against its evidence.** Read the data, code or source behind it, and look for what would disprove it.
3. **Ask.** Tie every question to a claim and its location.

| Kind | Example |
|---|---|
| How was it measured | How many runs, under what conditions, what was left out? |
| Why this way | What else did you consider, and why not that? |
| What are the limits | What happens beyond the range you tested? |
| Whose work | Show me where this is in the code, and explain it. |
| What does it mean | Does this result really support that conclusion? |
| AI use | Which parts did AI help with, and how did you check them? |

4. **Rehearse if asked.** One question at a time. Compare the student's answer with the evidence, and say what was weak in it.

## Answers

Give what the evidence supports and where to find it, and name what the student must check or decide. Do not write a script to memorize: the committee tests whether they understand the work. Never give an answer that relies on a result that does not exist. If a claim has no evidence, say so plainly: the fix is in the thesis, not in the answer.

Never predict the outcome of the defense.

## Record

Write to `docs/defense-notes.md`: each question, the claim and its location, what the evidence supports, and what remains open. Leave the thesis files as they are; weak claims go to `bachelor-thesis-writing` as suggestions.
